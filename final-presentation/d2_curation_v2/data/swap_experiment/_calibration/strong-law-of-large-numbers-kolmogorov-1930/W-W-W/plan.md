1. Center the sequence by setting \(Y_i = X_i - \mu\), and prove that \(E(Y_1)=0\) while each \(Y_i\) remains integrable and independent identically distributed.

2. Rewrite the desired limit for \(\frac{S_n}{n}\) as the corresponding limit for the centered partial sums \(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^n Y_k\), so that it is enough to prove convergence to \(0\).

3. Prove the strong law in the zero-mean case: for integrable i.i.d. variables with mean \(0\), the normalized partial sums converge to \(0\) almost surely.

4. Transfer the centered convergence back to the original sequence, using \(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^n X_k = \mu + \frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^n Y_k\), to conclude almost sure convergence to \(\mu\).
