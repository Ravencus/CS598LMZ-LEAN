1. Show that for every \(y \in (-1,1)\), \(y\) is an accumulation point of the sequence \(\sin n\); equivalently, \(\{\sin n : n \in \mathbb N\}\) is dense in \((-1,1)\).

2. Show that every accumulation point \(y\) of the sequence \(\sin n\) satisfies \(-1 \le y \le 1\), using that \(\sin n \in [-1,1]\) for all \(n\).

3. Deduce that \(1\) is an accumulation point of \(\sin n\): since points of \((-1,1)\) arbitrarily close to \(1\) are accumulation points and all accumulation points lie in the closed interval \([-1,1]\), the endpoint \(1\) is also an accumulation point.

4. Deduce similarly that \(-1\) is an accumulation point of \(\sin n\).

5. Conclude that the set of accumulation points of \(\sin n\) is contained in \([-1,1]\) and contains both endpoints, so its least upper bound is \(1\) and its greatest lower bound is \(-1\).

6. Unfold the definitions of `seqLimsup` and `seqLiminf` as the supremum and infimum of the set of accumulation points, and infer \(\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sin n = 1\) and \(\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sin n = -1\).
