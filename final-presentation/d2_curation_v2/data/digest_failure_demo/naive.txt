The proof strategy reframes the problem from its original notation into a geometric one: think of the objects as vectors, and look for a subset whose directions align enough to prevent too much cancellation. It then studies how to certify such alignment by projecting vectors onto carefully chosen directions, because a large positive total projection immediately gives a lower bound on the norm of the subset sum. To find a good direction, the argument first explores discrete partitions of directions or coordinates as coarse ways to force concentration, and then upgrades this to averaging over all directions, showing that some direction must perform at least as well as the average. Finally, it tests sharpness by constructing near-extremal configurations, so the method does not just prove existence of a bound but helps identify the best possible one.
