Let
\[
S_N=1+\frac1{2!}+\frac1{3!}+\cdots+\frac1{N!},
\]
where \(N\) is a positive integer. We will prove that
\[
S_N<3
\]
for every positive integer \(N\).

Note first that every term is positive, so \(S_N\) is well-defined.

Now for each integer \(k\ge 2\), we have
\[
k!=1\cdot 2\cdot 3\cdots k.
\]
Since every factor from \(2\) onward is at least \(2\), we get
\[
k! \ge 2^{k-1}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\frac1{k!}\le \frac1{2^{k-1}}.
\]

Applying this to each term in the sum,
\[
S_N
=1+\frac1{2!}+\frac1{3!}+\cdots+\frac1{N!}
\le
1+\frac12+\frac1{2^2}+\cdots+\frac1{2^{N-1}}.
\]

The right-hand side is a finite geometric series with ratio \(1/2\), so
\[
1+\frac12+\frac1{2^2}+\cdots+\frac1{2^{N-1}}
<1+\frac12+\frac1{2^2}+\cdots
= \sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac1{2^j}=2.
\]
Hence
\[
S_N<2.
\]

Since \(2<3\), it follows immediately that
\[
S_N<3.
\]

Thus, for every positive integer \(N\),
\[
1+\frac1{2!}+\cdots+\frac1{N!}<3.
\]

**Final answer:** For every positive integer \(N\),
\[
1+\frac{1}{2!}+\cdots+\frac{1}{N!}<3.
\]
In fact, the stronger inequality
\[
1+\frac{1}{2!}+\cdots+\frac{1}{N!}<2
\]
holds.
