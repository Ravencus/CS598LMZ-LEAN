For
\[
J_n=\int_0^1 \frac{\log(1+x/n)}{x}\,dx,
\]
we want
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty} n\,|nJ_n-1|.
\]

Since \(\log(1+t)<t\) for every \(t>0\), we have
\[
\frac{\log(1+x/n)}{x}<\frac1n \qquad (0<x\le 1),
\]
hence
\[
J_n<\frac1n.
\]
So \(nJ_n-1<0\), and therefore
\[
|nJ_n-1|=1-nJ_n.
\]
Thus it suffices to compute
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty} n(1-nJ_n).
\]

**Step 1: Expand \(J_n\) into a series.**

For \(n\ge 2\) and \(x\in[0,1]\), we have \(|x/n|\le 1/2\), so the power series
\[
\log(1+u)=\sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{k}u^k
\]
converges uniformly for \(u=x/n\). Hence we may integrate termwise:
\[
\frac{\log(1+x/n)}{x}
=
\sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{k}\frac{x^{k-1}}{n^k}.
\]
Therefore
\[
J_n
=
\sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{k n^k}\int_0^1 x^{k-1}\,dx
=
\sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{k^2 n^k}.
\]
So
\[
nJ_n
=
\sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{k^2 n^{k-1}}
=
1-\frac{1}{4n}+\frac{1}{9n^2}-\frac{1}{16n^3}+\cdots.
\]

Hence
\[
1-nJ_n
=
\frac{1}{4n}-\frac{1}{9n^2}+\frac{1}{16n^3}-\cdots,
\]
and multiplying by \(n\),
\[
n(1-nJ_n)
=
\frac14-\frac{1}{9n}+\frac{1}{16n^2}-\cdots.
\]

This already suggests the limit is \(1/4\). To make it rigorous:

\[
n(1-nJ_n)-\frac14
=
\sum_{k=2}^\infty \frac{(-1)^k}{k^2 n^{k-2}}.
\]
Hence
\[
\left|n(1-nJ_n)-\frac14\right|
\le
\sum_{k=3}^\infty \frac{1}{k^2 n^{k-2}}
\le
\frac1n\sum_{k=3}^\infty \frac1{k^2}.
\]
The right-hand side tends to \(0\) as \(n\to\infty\). Therefore
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty} n(1-nJ_n)=\frac14.
\]

Since \(|nJ_n-1|=1-nJ_n\), we conclude
\[
\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty} n\,|nJ_n-1|=\frac14.}
\]
