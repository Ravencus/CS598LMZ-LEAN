Let
\[
S_n=\sum_{i=1}^n \frac1{n+\sqrt i}.
\]
(If the sum starts at \(i=0\), the extra term is \(1/n=O(n^{-1})\), which does not affect the required precision.)

We prove
\[
S_n=1-\frac{2}{3}\frac1{\sqrt n}+o(n^{-1/2}).
\]

**Step 1: Expand each summand uniformly.**

Write
\[
\frac1{n+\sqrt i}=\frac1n\cdot \frac1{1+\frac{\sqrt i}{n}}.
\]
Set
\[
x_i:=\frac{\sqrt i}{n}.
\]
For \(1\le i\le n\),
\[
0\le x_i\le \frac{\sqrt n}{n}=\frac1{\sqrt n}\le 1
\]
for all large \(n\).

Now
\[
\frac1{1+x}=1-x+\frac{x^2}{1+x},
\]
hence for \(x\ge 0\),
\[
\left|\frac1{1+x}-(1-x)\right|=\frac{x^2}{1+x}\le x^2.
\]
Therefore, uniformly in \(1\le i\le n\),
\[
\frac1{n+\sqrt i}
=\frac1n-\frac{\sqrt i}{n^2}+r_{i,n},
\qquad |r_{i,n}|\le \frac{i}{n^3}.
\]

Summing over \(i\le n\),
\[
S_n
=\sum_{i=1}^n \frac1n-\frac1{n^2}\sum_{i=1}^n \sqrt i+\sum_{i=1}^n r_{i,n}.
\]
Thus
\[
S_n
=1-\frac1{n^2}\sum_{i=1}^n \sqrt i+R_n,
\]
with
\[
|R_n|\le \sum_{i=1}^n \frac{i}{n^3}
=\frac{n(n+1)}{2n^3}
=O(n^{-1}).
\]
Since \(n^{-1}=o(n^{-1/2})\), this remainder is small enough.

**Step 2: Estimate \(\sum_{i=1}^n \sqrt i\).**

Because \(x\mapsto \sqrt x\) is increasing,
\[
\int_0^n \sqrt x\,dx \le \sum_{i=1}^n \sqrt i \le \int_1^{n+1}\sqrt x\,dx.
\]
Hence
\[
\frac23 n^{3/2}\le \sum_{i=1}^n \sqrt i \le \frac23\big((n+1)^{3/2}-1\big).
\]
So
\[
\sum_{i=1}^n \sqrt i=\frac23 n^{3/2}+O(\sqrt n).
\]
Dividing by \(n^2\),
\[
\frac1{n^2}\sum_{i=1}^n \sqrt i
=\frac23\,n^{-1/2}+O(n^{-3/2}).
\]

**Step 3: Combine the estimates.**

From Step 1,
\[
S_n
=1-\frac1{n^2}\sum_{i=1}^n \sqrt i+O(n^{-1}),
\]
so by Step 2,
\[
S_n
=1-\frac23\,n^{-1/2}+O(n^{-1}).
\]
Since \(O(n^{-1})=o(n^{-1/2})\), we get
\[
S_n=1-\frac{2}{3}\frac1{\sqrt n}+o\!\left(\frac1{\sqrt n}\right).
\]

**Final answer**
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\sum_{i\le n}\frac1{n+\sqrt i}
=1-\frac{2}{3}\frac1{\sqrt n}+o\!\left(\frac1{\sqrt n}\right)}
\]
as \(n\to\infty\).
