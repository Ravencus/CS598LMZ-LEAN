Let  
\[
H_n=\sum_{k=1}^n \frac1k.
\]
We want to prove
\[
H_n\ge \log n+\frac{n+1}{2n}.
\]

The key point is to use the convexity of \(f(x)=\frac1x\) on \((0,\infty)\).

**Step 1: A convexity estimate on each interval.**

Since
\[
f''(x)=\frac{2}{x^3}>0 \qquad (x>0),
\]
the function \(f(x)=1/x\) is convex on \((0,\infty)\).

For a convex function \(f\), the graph lies below each secant line, so on every interval \([k,k+1]\),
\[
f(x)\le \frac{k+1-x}{1}f(k)+\frac{x-k}{1}f(k+1)
\qquad (x\in[k,k+1]).
\]
Integrating from \(k\) to \(k+1\), we get
\[
\int_k^{k+1} f(x)\,dx
\le \frac{f(k)+f(k+1)}{2}.
\]
Applying this to \(f(x)=1/x\),
\[
\int_k^{k+1}\frac{dx}{x}\le \frac12\left(\frac1k+\frac1{k+1}\right).
\]

Since \(\int_k^{k+1}\frac{dx}{x}=\log\frac{k+1}{k}\), this gives
\[
\log\frac{k+1}{k}\le \frac12\left(\frac1k+\frac1{k+1}\right).
\]

**Step 2: Sum these inequalities.**

Summing for \(k=1,\dots,n-1\),
\[
\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\log\frac{k+1}{k}
\le \frac12\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\left(\frac1k+\frac1{k+1}\right).
\]
The left-hand side telescopes:
\[
\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\log\frac{k+1}{k}=\log n.
\]
The right-hand side is
\[
\frac12\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac1k+\sum_{k=2}^{n}\frac1k\right)
=\frac12\left(H_{n-1}+(H_n-1)\right).
\]
Since \(H_{n-1}=H_n-\frac1n\), this becomes
\[
\log n\le \frac12\left(H_n-\frac1n+H_n-1\right)
=H_n-\frac12\left(1+\frac1n\right).
\]
Therefore
\[
H_n\ge \log n+\frac12\left(1+\frac1n\right)
=\log n+\frac{n+1}{2n}.
\]

This is exactly the desired bound.

It is stronger than the monotonicity bound
\[
H_n\ge \log n+\frac1n,
\]
because
\[
\frac{n+1}{2n}-\frac1n=\frac{n-1}{2n}\ge 0.
\]

**Final answer.**
\[
\boxed{\,H_n=\sum_{k=1}^n \frac1k \ge \log n+\frac{n+1}{2n}\,}
\]
for every integer \(n\ge 1\). This follows by applying convexity of \(x\mapsto 1/x\) on each interval \([k,k+1]\) and summing the resulting trapezoidal estimates.
