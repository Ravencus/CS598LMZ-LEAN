Let  
\[
S:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(x_k-x_{k-1})(1-x_k),
\qquad 0=x_0<x_1<\cdots<x_n<1.
\]

We estimate \(S\).

For each \(k\),
\[
(x_k-x_{k-1})(1-x_k)
= (x_k-x_{k-1})\bigl((1-x_k)\bigr).
\]
Since \(x_{k-1}<x_k\), we have
\[
1-x_k<1-x_{k-1}.
\]
Hence
\[
(x_k-x_{k-1})(1-x_k)
<
(x_k-x_{k-1})(1-x_{k-1}).
\]
Therefore
\[
2(x_k-x_{k-1})(1-x_k)
<
(x_k-x_{k-1})\bigl((1-x_k)+(1-x_{k-1})\bigr).
\]
Now note that
\[
(1-x_k)+(1-x_{k-1})=2-(x_k+x_{k-1}),
\]
so
\[
(x_k-x_{k-1})\bigl((1-x_k)+(1-x_{k-1})\bigr)
=(x_k-x_{k-1})(2-x_k-x_{k-1}).
\]
But
\[
(x_k-x_{k-1})(2-x_k-x_{k-1})
=(x_k-x_{k-1})\bigl((1-x_{k-1})+(1-x_k)\bigr)
=(1-x_{k-1})^2-(1-x_k)^2.
\]
Thus
\[
2(x_k-x_{k-1})(1-x_k)
<
(1-x_{k-1})^2-(1-x_k)^2.
\]

Summing from \(k=1\) to \(n\),
\[
2S
<
\sum_{k=1}^n \Bigl((1-x_{k-1})^2-(1-x_k)^2\Bigr).
\]
The sum telescopes:
\[
\sum_{k=1}^n \Bigl((1-x_{k-1})^2-(1-x_k)^2\Bigr)
=(1-x_0)^2-(1-x_n)^2.
\]
Since \(x_0=0\),
\[
2S<1-(1-x_n)^2.
\]
Hence
\[
S<\frac{1-(1-x_n)^2}{2}.
\]

Because \(0<x_n<1\), we have \((1-x_n)^2>0\), so
\[
\frac{1-(1-x_n)^2}{2}<\frac12.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{\sum_{k=1}^{n}(x_k-x_{k-1})(1-x_k)<\frac12.}
\]

A slightly sharper bound is
\[
\boxed{\sum_{k=1}^{n}(x_k-x_{k-1})(1-x_k)<\frac{1-(1-x_n)^2}{2}
= x_n-\frac{x_n^2}{2}.}
\]

**Final answer:**  
\[
\sum_{k=1}^{n}(x_k-x_{k-1})(1-x_k)
<\frac{1-(1-x_n)^2}{2}<\frac12.
\]
