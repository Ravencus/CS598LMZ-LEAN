Let  
\[
S_n=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\log\!\left(1+\frac{k}{n^2}\right).
\]
We compute \(\lim_{n\to\infty} S_n\).

Since \(\frac{k}{n^2}\ge 0\), we can use the standard inequality
\[
0\le \log(1+x)\le x \qquad (x\ge 0).
\]
Applying this with \(x=\frac{k}{n^2}\), we get
\[
0\le \log\!\left(1+\frac{k}{n^2}\right)\le \frac{k}{n^2}.
\]
Summing from \(k=1\) to \(n\),
\[
0\le S_n\le \sum_{k=1}^n \frac{k}{n^2}
= \frac{1}{n^2}\sum_{k=1}^n k
= \frac{1}{n^2}\cdot \frac{n(n+1)}{2}
= \frac{n+1}{2n}.
\]
This bound alone is not enough to force convergence to \(0\), so we sharpen it.

Use the Taylor estimate near \(0\):
\[
\log(1+x)=x+O(x^2)\qquad (x\to 0).
\]
More explicitly, for \(x\ge 0\),
\[
0\le x-\log(1+x)\le \frac{x^2}{2}.
\]
Hence
\[
\log(1+x)\le x.
\]
So
\[
S_n\le \sum_{k=1}^n \frac{k}{n^2}
= \frac{n+1}{2n},
\]
but more importantly we can compare the full sum to the linear term:
\[
S_n
= \sum_{k=1}^n \left(\frac{k}{n^2}+O\!\left(\frac{k^2}{n^4}\right)\right).
\]
Thus
\[
S_n
= \frac{1}{n^2}\sum_{k=1}^n k
+ O\!\left(\frac{1}{n^4}\sum_{k=1}^n k^2\right).
\]
Now
\[
\frac{1}{n^2}\sum_{k=1}^n k
= \frac{n(n+1)}{2n^2}
= \frac{n+1}{2n},
\]
and
\[
\frac{1}{n^4}\sum_{k=1}^n k^2
= \frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6n^4}
= O\!\left(\frac1n\right).
\]
So this asymptotic would suggest a limit of \(1/2\). Let us verify this rigorously.

Indeed, since
\[
\max_{1\le k\le n}\frac{k}{n^2}=\frac1n\to 0,
\]
the approximation \(\log(1+x)\sim x\) is uniform over \(x=\frac{k}{n^2}\). Define
\[
\varepsilon_n=\sup_{0\le x\le 1/n}\left|\frac{\log(1+x)}{x}-1\right|.
\]
Then \(\varepsilon_n\to 0\), and for every \(1\le k\le n\),
\[
\log\!\left(1+\frac{k}{n^2}\right)=\frac{k}{n^2}(1+\delta_{k,n}),
\qquad |\delta_{k,n}|\le \varepsilon_n.
\]
Therefore
\[
S_n
=\sum_{k=1}^n \frac{k}{n^2}(1+\delta_{k,n})
=\frac{1}{n^2}\sum_{k=1}^n k
+\sum_{k=1}^n \frac{k}{n^2}\delta_{k,n}.
\]
The error term satisfies
\[
\left|\sum_{k=1}^n \frac{k}{n^2}\delta_{k,n}\right|
\le \varepsilon_n \cdot \frac{1}{n^2}\sum_{k=1}^n k
= \varepsilon_n \cdot \frac{n+1}{2n}\to 0.
\]
Hence
\[
S_n-\frac{1}{n^2}\sum_{k=1}^n k \to 0.
\]
Since
\[
\frac{1}{n^2}\sum_{k=1}^n k
=\frac{n(n+1)}{2n^2}
=\frac{n+1}{2n}\to \frac12,
\]
it follows that
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty} \sum_{k=1}^{n}\log\!\left(1+\frac{k}{n^2}\right)=\frac12.
\]

Final answer:
\[
\boxed{\frac12}.
\]
