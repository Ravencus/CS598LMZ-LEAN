Let  
\[
S_n=\sum_{k=1}^n a_k,\qquad a_n>0,\qquad p>1,
\]
and
\[
T_N=\sum_{n=1}^N \frac{a_n}{S_n^p}.
\]

Since \(a_n>0\), the sequence \((S_n)\) is strictly increasing and \(S_n>0\) for all \(n\).

We will show that \(T_N\) is increasing and uniformly bounded, hence convergent.

---

## Key estimate

Consider the function
\[
f(x)=x^{-p}\qquad (x>0).
\]
Since \(p>1\), \(f\) is positive and decreasing.

For each \(n\ge 2\), on the interval \([S_{n-1},S_n]\) we have
\[
f(S_n)\le f(x)\le f(S_{n-1}),
\]
so multiplying by the interval length \(S_n-S_{n-1}=a_n\),
\[
a_n\,S_n^{-p}\le \int_{S_{n-1}}^{S_n} x^{-p}\,dx \le a_n\,S_{n-1}^{-p}.
\]
Hence
\[
\frac{a_n}{S_n^p}\le \int_{S_{n-1}}^{S_n} x^{-p}\,dx.
\]
Now compute the integral:
\[
\int_{S_{n-1}}^{S_n} x^{-p}\,dx
=
\left[\frac{x^{1-p}}{1-p}\right]_{S_{n-1}}^{S_n}
=
\frac{S_{n-1}^{\,1-p}-S_n^{\,1-p}}{p-1}.
\]
Therefore, for every \(n\ge 2\),
\[
\frac{a_n}{S_n^p}
\le
\frac{S_{n-1}^{\,1-p}-S_n^{\,1-p}}{p-1}.
\]

---

## Sum the inequalities

Summing from \(n=2\) to \(N\),
\[
\sum_{n=2}^N \frac{a_n}{S_n^p}
\le
\frac{1}{p-1}\sum_{n=2}^N\left(S_{n-1}^{\,1-p}-S_n^{\,1-p}\right).
\]
The sum telescopes:
\[
\sum_{n=2}^N \frac{a_n}{S_n^p}
\le
\frac{S_1^{\,1-p}-S_N^{\,1-p}}{p-1}.
\]
Adding the first term,
\[
T_N
=
\frac{a_1}{S_1^p}+\sum_{n=2}^N \frac{a_n}{S_n^p}.
\]
But \(S_1=a_1\), so
\[
\frac{a_1}{S_1^p}=\frac{1}{S_1^{p-1}}=S_1^{\,1-p}.
\]
Thus
\[
T_N
\le
S_1^{\,1-p}+\frac{S_1^{\,1-p}-S_N^{\,1-p}}{p-1}
=
\frac{p\,S_1^{\,1-p}-S_N^{\,1-p}}{p-1}.
\]
In particular,
\[
0<T_N\le \frac{p}{p-1}\,S_1^{\,1-p}.
\]

Since each term is positive, \(T_N\) is increasing in \(N\). Since it is also bounded above, \((T_N)\) converges.

---

## Final answer

For every \(N\ge 1\),
\[
0<T_N=\sum_{n=1}^N \frac{a_n}{S_n^p}
\le
S_1^{\,1-p}+\frac{S_1^{\,1-p}-S_N^{\,1-p}}{p-1}
=
\frac{p\,S_1^{\,1-p}-S_N^{\,1-p}}{p-1}
\le
\frac{p}{p-1}S_1^{\,1-p}.
\]
Hence \((T_N)\) is increasing and bounded, so the series
\[
\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{a_n}{S_n^p}
\]
converges.
