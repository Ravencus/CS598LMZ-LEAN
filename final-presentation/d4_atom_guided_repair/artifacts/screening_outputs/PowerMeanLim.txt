Let
\[
M_p(x_1,\dots,x_n;w_1,\dots,w_n)
=\left(\sum_{i=1}^n w_i x_i^p\right)^{1/p},
\]
where \(x_i\ge 0\), \(w_i>0\), and \(\sum_{i=1}^n w_i=1\). We will prove that
\[
\lim_{p\to+\infty} M_p(x_1,\dots,x_n;w_1,\dots,w_n)=\max\{x_1,\dots,x_n\}.
\]

Set
\[
m:=\max\{x_1,\dots,x_n\}.
\]

We must show that \(M_p\to m\) as \(p\to\infty\).

## Step 1: Upper bound

Since each \(x_i\le m\), we have
\[
x_i^p\le m^p \qquad (i=1,\dots,n).
\]
Multiplying by \(w_i\) and summing gives
\[
\sum_{i=1}^n w_i x_i^p \le \sum_{i=1}^n w_i m^p = m^p\sum_{i=1}^n w_i = m^p.
\]
Therefore
\[
M_p=\left(\sum_{i=1}^n w_i x_i^p\right)^{1/p}\le (m^p)^{1/p}=m.
\]

So for every \(p>0\),
\[
M_p\le m.
\]

## Step 2: Lower bound

Because \(m=\max_i x_i\), there exists at least one index \(j\) such that \(x_j=m\). Then
\[
\sum_{i=1}^n w_i x_i^p \ge w_j x_j^p = w_j m^p.
\]
Taking the \(p\)-th root,
\[
M_p \ge (w_j m^p)^{1/p} = w_j^{1/p} m.
\]

Thus
\[
w_j^{1/p}m \le M_p \le m.
\]

## Step 3: Pass to the limit

Since \(w_j>0\), we know
\[
w_j^{1/p}\to 1 \qquad \text{as } p\to\infty.
\]
Hence
\[
w_j^{1/p}m \to m.
\]
Together with
\[
w_j^{1/p}m \le M_p \le m,
\]
the squeeze theorem yields
\[
\lim_{p\to\infty} M_p = m.
\]

That is,
\[
\boxed{\lim_{p\to +\infty} M_p(x_1,\dots,x_n;w_1,\dots,w_n)=\max\{x_1,\dots,x_n\}.}
\]

## Remark on the case \(m=0\)

If \(m=0\), then all \(x_i=0\), so \(M_p=0\) for all \(p\), and the conclusion is immediate.

## Final answer

\[
\boxed{\lim_{p\to +\infty}\left(\sum_{i=1}^n w_i x_i^p\right)^{1/p}=\max\{x_1,\dots,x_n\}.}
\]
The proof follows from the bounds
\[
w_j^{1/p}\max_i x_i \le M_p \le \max_i x_i,
\]
where \(j\) is any index such that \(x_j=\max_i x_i\), and from \(w_j^{1/p}\to 1\).
